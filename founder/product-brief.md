# LSW product brief: DROP 001

## Assumptions

- Assumption: the "small startup budget" is under $5,000 total for DROP 001. No number was given, so every cost-sensitive choice below is sized to that ceiling. Founder to confirm.
- Assumption: DROP 001 is a pre-order or small-batch run of 50 to 150 units per style, not stock bought up front. No production quantity exists yet.
- Assumption: the founder is the only team member and runs design, social and fulfilment. No team was mentioned.
- Assumption: no audience exists today (none was mentioned), so the first buyers come from the founder's personal network and new social content.
- Assumption: all target numbers below are proposed goals for the founder to accept or change. They are not benchmarks and not predictions.
- No `founder/persona-gen.md` exists. Run `/founder:persona-gen` for full buyer personas.

## 1. Problem statement

Fashion-aware young buyers who are Christian or faith-curious have two bad options. Overtly Christian apparel usually looks like merch: slogans, soft fits, little design ambition. Mainstream streetwear looks right but says nothing about faith, discipline or purpose. A buyer who wants both has to pick one identity or the other.

- Who feels it most: a buyer who follows fashion, music, social media and sneaker culture and wants a faith message that does not look like church merch.
- How they cope today: they wear mainstream streetwear and keep faith separate, or buy faith apparel they find less stylish.
- Evidence status: this is the founder's thesis. No buyer has confirmed it yet. Assumption 1 in section 6 tests it.

## 2. Target audience

Primary audience, in one sentence: Christian and faith-open buyers in their late teens to late twenties who follow streetwear drops, sneakers and music on social media, and who would pay premium prices for heavyweight, oversized pieces that do not look like church merch.

- The age band (late teens to late twenties) is an assumption. The founder said "younger customers".
- Secondary audience: streetwear buyers with no faith connection who buy for the look. The founder wants designs to stay relevant to the broader market, so this group must not be turned away by the message.

## 3. Value proposition

LSW helps young, fashion-aware buyers wear their faith, discipline and purpose by making dark, oversized, heavyweight limited-edition streetwear with original cross and star motifs, unlike faith apparel brands that lead with slogans and mainstream streetwear brands that carry no meaning.

## 4. Core features (MVP)

Six items. The product and the store are both in scope because the founder's goal is a real store that takes orders.

1. **Signature hoodie.** Heavyweight, oversized, washed black or charcoal, restrained Core decoration. It is the hero product and the one that anchors the brand look. The drop has no identity without it.
2. **Oversized graphic tee.** Lowest price point on the line, with a cross or star graphic. It gives first-time buyers a cheap way in and gives the founder the most affordable item to produce and test.
3. **Baggy sweatpants.** Completes a set with the hoodie, which raises order value. Part of the founder's starting three-product recommendation.
4. **Limited-run mechanic.** A fixed quantity per style, shown on the product page, with a numbered count or "sold out" state. Without it the brand is not limited-edition, and the premium price loses its reason.
5. **Working online store.** Product pages, size selection, a bag, real checkout, order confirmation emails. The current preview site has no connected checkout or sign-up, so this is the biggest build gap.
6. **Brand packaging set.** Neck labels, woven labels, care labels, hang tags and a mailer. The founder listed these as part of the goal, and they are what separates a premium garment from blank stock with a print.

Held back for later: statement hoodie, Special Edition pieces, baggy jeans and beanie. Each adds sizing, sourcing or crystal-work cost before the base line is validated. The founder's own recommendation is hoodie, tee and sweatpants pending validation, and this brief agrees.

## 5. Success metrics (first 90 days)

Proposed targets, to be set by the founder once the budget and run size are confirmed.

| KPI | Type | Proposed 90-day target |
|---|---|---|
| Email or SMS sign-ups before the drop | Leading | 300 |
| Pre-orders or paid orders at the opening price | Leading, then revenue | 60 orders |
| Sell-through of the limited run | Demand signal | 70% of units inside 14 days of opening |
| Store conversion rate (orders / sessions) | Leading | 2% or higher |
| Repeat or referral signal | Retention | 10 of the first 60 buyers post, tag or reorder |

Revenue is deliberately not the lead number. With no price confirmed, revenue targets would be invented. Set a revenue target after the CFO step produces a confirmed unit cost and price.

## 6. Risks and assumptions to test

Each test costs under $500 and runs in under 2 weeks.

1. **Buyers want faith and streetwear in one garment.**
   - Test: post 3 DROP 001 concept mockups on Instagram and TikTok, and ask for sign-ups with a link in the bio. Spend up to $150 on ads to the same page in a small test group.
   - Pass: 100 sign-ups in 14 days from people outside the founder's immediate circle.
   - Fail: fewer than 30. Revisit how loudly the faith message leads.
2. **Buyers will pay a premium price for a brand with no track record.**
   - Test: open a pre-order page for the hoodie only, at a stated price, with a deposit or full payment, capped at 20 units. No unit is produced until the cap is reached.
   - Pass: 10 paid pre-orders in 14 days.
   - Fail: fewer than 3 paid pre-orders. The price or the proof is wrong.
   - Price is not set. Run `/founder:pricing-strategy` before this test.
3. **A supplier can deliver heavyweight, oversized quality at a cost that leaves margin.**
   - Test: request quotes from 3 suppliers and order 1 sample hoodie from the best 2, about $150 to $300 for samples.
   - Pass: a landed unit cost the founder can price a premium product against. No supplier prices exist yet, so none are stated here.
   - Fail: no sample matches the heavyweight, oversized spec within budget.

Also unchecked, and a launch blocker: the LSW trademark and commercial availability. Run this check before any money goes into labels or packaging.

## 7. Go-to-market snapshot

CAC figures below are estimates built from stated assumptions, not sourced benchmarks. Replace them with real numbers after the first test.

1. **Organic short-form video and Instagram (founder-made content).**
   - Estimate: $0 to $10 per order in cash. Arithmetic: $0 ad spend, about 10 hours a week of founder time, assumed 20 orders from it. Time cost is excluded.
2. **Paid Meta and TikTok ads to the sign-up page, then to the drop.**
   - Estimate: $20 to $40 per order. Arithmetic: assumed $300 ad budget over the pre-order window divided by 8 to 15 attributed orders. Real ad costs vary by audience and year, so treat this as a placeholder.
3. **Micro-creators and church or youth-group networks (gifted product for posts).**
   - Estimate: $15 to $35 per order. Arithmetic: product cost of 5 gifted hoodies (unit cost not yet known) divided by an assumed 5 to 10 resulting orders.

**Growth tactic specific to LSW:** numbered limited editions. Each garment carries a number (for example 014 of 100) on the woven label and the buyer can claim it publicly. This turns each buyer's post into proof of scarcity, and the number is a reason to share and to come back for DROP 002. It costs one label print line, not a campaign.
