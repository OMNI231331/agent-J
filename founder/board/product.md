# Product lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The brief has a clear core: "the one thing" is a heavyweight hoodie with a striking back graphic and a quiet front mark.
- The design logic is coherent: small front mark, large back statement, restrained crystals. The brand is already visually disciplined.
- The three-piece scope is focused. The founder has already said no to jeans, sweatpants and a heavy-embellishment piece for now.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The experience is not written yet.** The lens asks what the first five minutes with the product feel like: unboxing, first wear, first wash. None of that is designed or sampled. The packaging and care card are concepts only.
2. **Craft risk hidden in the details.** Rhinestone outlines on heavyweight fleece can crack, lose stones in the wash, or stiffen the garment. This is the most likely place for a bad first review. It is untested.
3. **Seams between touchpoints.** Ordering, shipping, returns and the size guide are not yet connected to the product. Size guidance is blank until a real sample is measured.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Describe the moment a customer opens the box and puts the hoodie on in one sentence. What do they feel?
- If you could cut one element from the hoodie and keep the brand, what would it be?
- Has a real garment been measured and washed with the crystals on? What survived?
- What is the returns and exchange experience, and who handles it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Produce one physical sample of the Signature Hoodie and run the wash and stone-pull test before any pre-order.
- Write the unboxing and first-wear experience as a one-page brief and check each touchpoint against it.
- Keep the crystal panel off seams and high-friction areas, as the design notes already say.
