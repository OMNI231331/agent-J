# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The range is small and tight: 8 products, each with a clear job. That is focus.
- The Heavyweight Tee at $38 (240gsm organic cotton, boxy) is a clear anchor. It can be described in one sentence.
- Product descriptions name specific materials (brushed fleece, rigid denim, french terry). That is where craft starts to show.
- The storefront already exists, so the first-five-minutes experience can be designed now.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. No product exists yet in the customer's hands. There are no supplier quotes, no samples and no real photos. The brief makes quality claims no one has verified, and the details nobody sees (stitching, shrinkage, seams) are untested.
2. The experience is not whole. Checkout is not connected, so the path from ad to payment to receipt to delivery to follow-up has seams. Every handoff (store, payment, factory, shipping, returns) is unowned.
3. The one thing is not chosen. Eight products across tees, denim, jackets and a cap is wide for a student founder. Without a single hero, the pitch is "basics, good fabric, fair price", which needs a paragraph.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is Northline, and what is the one product it must nail?
- Describe the moment a customer opens the box. What do they see, feel and read?
- Which of the 8 products would you cut first, and why is it still there?
- Who makes each product, and what are the real unit costs, minimum orders and lead times? (Open: no numbers in the brief.)
- Have you held a finished sample? What failed in it?
- What happens when a customer needs the wrong size swapped? Who handles it?
- Who is the customer? The brief marks the 18-34 audience as an assumption.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Cut to one hero product (likely the tee) plus at most two others for launch.
- Get physical samples from the factory and test wash, fit and wear before selling.
- Shoot real photos of real samples; no mockups.
- Connect checkout and run a full test order through delivery, receipt and a returns request.
- Write the one-sentence pitch and test that a stranger can repeat it back.
