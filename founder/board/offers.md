# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The product idea has a clear identity hook: a crystal-outlined cross on the back of a heavyweight hoodie is distinctive and photographs well.
- The three-piece first drop (hoodie, tee, beanie) is a sensible, small range with a low-price entry point.
- The faith-and-discipline message gives buyers a reason to care beyond the garment.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No market picked yet.** The lens starts with the market: is the pain massive, do buyers have money, can they be reached, is it growing? The founder has not named a target buyer, so none of the four can be scored. Nothing is known about how big the faith-aligned streetwear buyer group is.
2. **Likelihood of value is unproven.** Buyers need to believe the piece will look and feel premium. Nothing physical has been sampled, so fabric weight, crystal durability and fit are claims, not proof.
3. **No offer, only a product.** The site has a draft price and no guarantee, bonus, launch-week incentive or reason to buy now. Without urgency built on a real limit, it is a product page.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who is the first 100 buyers, specifically: age, where they spend time online, and what they already wear?
- What outcome do they buy: feeling of identity, status, comfort, or faith expression? Which one is the offer's core promise?
- What proof can you show before a sale: real sample photos, wash tests, a size guide from a real garment?
- What is the guarantee you can afford (for example, free exchange on sizing)? The CFO will need the number.
- What is real scarcity? A stated run size, only once a production count exists.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Name the target buyer in one sentence and check it against the consumer panel.
- Produce a real sample of the hoodie and check the crystal wash durability before taking pre-orders.
- Write a guarantee and an exchange policy the CFO can cost.
- Only show "limited" or "sold out" language when a real production count backs it.
