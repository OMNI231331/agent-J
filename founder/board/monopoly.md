# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The brand has a distinct symbol and a message that is hard to copy word for word.
- Faith-centered positioning can build loyalty inside a community that other streetwear brands ignore, which is a possible small market to own first.
- Limited drops create a reason to buy now and a reason to return.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Crowded adjacent market with no proof of a niche.** Heavyweight oversized streetwear, graphic tees and hoodies are sold by many brands. Nothing has been mapped yet (the competitor scout has not been run), so the claim that LSW's space is open is untested.
2. **Distribution is unsolved.** The lens says a product without a way to reach buyers is not a business. Nothing yet says how the first 100 customers arrive or what each costs.
3. **Easy to copy visually.** A spiked cross and a chrome wordmark are design choices any competitor can imitate. The durable asset would have to be the community and the message, and that is not built yet.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which small market can LSW own first: a faith community, a specific city or school, or a particular online scene?
- How will the first 100 customers arrive, and what can each cost? Is there a community you already belong to?
- What do you know about this market that others don't? (The "secret.")
- What stops a copycat in month six: a community, a fan base, exclusive drops, or nothing?
- Trademark: has "LSW" been searched for apparel? A crowded name is a hidden moat killer.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Run /founder-competitors and map who sells faith-inspired and oversized streetwear, and at what prices.
- Name the first community to sell to and show one real channel to reach it.
- Run a trademark search for LSW in apparel before the brand is printed on anything.
