# LSW pricing strategy: DROP 001

## Assumptions

Every cost below is an assumption, not a supplier quote. No supplier quotes exist yet (facts.md). Replace each with a real quote before launch.

- Assumption: run size is 50 units per style, and the founder confirms the budget ceiling of $5,000 (product-brief.md).
- Assumption: 80% of produced units sell. The rest are written off at landed cost.
- Assumption: LSW pays outbound shipping (free shipping), and exchanges only, no cash refunds.
- Assumption: payment fee is 2.9% + $0.30 per order. Not verified this session.
- Assumption: sales mix is 40% hoodie, 40% tee, 20% sweatpants.
- Competitor prices are reused from `founder/competitor-matrix.md`, checked 2026-10-08 (under 30 days old). Most are reseller prices, not brand stores.

## 1. Pricing model analysis

| Model | Fit (1-5) | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed price per piece, set per drop | 5 | Simple, matches how streetwear is bought | Wrong price is stuck for the whole run |
| Tiered by decoration (Core / Signature / Special Edition) | 5 | Price tracks real cost: stones and embroidery add labor | More SKUs, more sizes to stock |
| Pre-order at drop price | 4 | Buys only what sells, cuts unsold stock | Slower shipping, buyer trust needed with no track record |
| Bundle or set | 3 | Lifts order value, one shipment | Discount eats margin |
| Early-access membership | 2 | Rewards repeat buyers | No audience yet to reward |
| Auction or dynamic pricing | 1 | Captures hype | Reads as a gimmick for a faith-centered brand |

**Recommendation:** fixed price per piece, tiered by decoration, sold as a pre-order with a hard cap. Because each tier maps to a real cost step and the cap is a real production limit, not a marketing device.

## 4. Unit economics (all costs Assumption)

Per unit, hoodie: blank $28.00, decoration $17.00 (print and embroidery $5, rhinestones and application $12), labels and tag $1.80, inbound freight $2.00. Landed: 28 + 17 + 1.80 + 2 = $48.80.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Price (launch) | $135.00 | $55.00 | $100.00 |
| Landed cost per unit produced | $48.80 | $15.80 | $30.30 |

Tee landed: blank $9.00, print $4.00, labels $1.80, freight $1.00 = $15.80. Pants landed: blank $20.00, decoration $7.00, labels $1.80, freight $1.50 = $30.30.

At scale prices ($150, $60, $115) contribution rises to $64.85 (43.2%), $27.61 (46.0%) and $57.09 (49.6%).

**Cash needed.** Estimate: 50 hoodies at $48.80 = $2,440, 50 tees at $15.80 = $790, 50 pants at $30.30 = $1,515. Total $4,745. That is 95% of the assumed $5,000 ceiling, leaving $255 for samples, store and ads. This is the tightest constraint. Pre-order the hoodie first (20-unit cap, per product-brief test 2) so cash is not tied up before demand shows.

**Break-even.** Fixed launch costs, Assumption: samples $300, label setup and screens $400, store and email tools $200, ads $300 = $1,200. Blended contribution: 0.4 x 51.19 + 0.4 x 23.05 + 0.2 x 43.42 = $38.38. Break-even: 1,200 / 38.38 = 31.3, so 32 units sold. Blended order value: 0.4 x 135 + 0.4 x 55 + 0.2 x 100 = $96.

## 5. Pricing psychology

1. **Anchoring.** Show the Special Edition hoodie at $225 on the drop page as the top rung, next to the $135 Signature. It is only an anchor if it is real: produce the 30 or do not list it. Because Sp5der sells at $220 to $225, $225 is believable.
2. **Decoy.** The Core hoodie at $110 makes the $135 Signature look like $25 for the crystal cross. It is a real product, not a fake price, so build it only when it ships.
3. **Set framing.** Hoodie plus pants at $225 instead of $235. Estimate: the $10 discount costs $10, but one box saves about $5 of shipping (Assumption: $11 for one box versus $8.50 + $7.50 = $16), net cost about $5 per set.

**No fake scarcity.** Show the real cap per style and a live count. Print edition numbers only if the run is numbered (lib/lsw.ts already says this). No countdown timers that reset, no "only 3 left" unless 3 are left. Do not restock a sold-out Special Edition under the same name.

## 6. Launch vs scale pricing

- **Launch price (first 90 days, DROP 001):** $135 hoodie, $55 tee, $100 pants. Because there is no track record, the price must be one buyers can justify, and $135 sits inside the sourced $108 to $150 band.
- **Test before committing:** a 20-unit hoodie pre-order at $135 (pass: 10 paid in 14 days, fail: fewer than 3, from product-brief.md). If it fails, test $120 before changing anything else.
- **Grandfathering:** buyers of DROP 001 keep what they paid, and get early access to DROP 002 for 24 hours. Never discount a sold-out drop afterward, because that punishes the first buyers.
- **Price increases, specific triggers:**
  - Raise the next drop's hoodie to $150 if 90% of units sell within 14 days of opening.
  - Raise the tee to $60 under the same trigger.
  - Hold prices if sell-through is 60% to 89%.
  - If under 60% after 60 days, make one markdown of at most 20%, labeled as final, and fix the cost or the offer before DROP 002. Sell-through below 60% costs more than any price cut (see section 4).
- **Review after quotes:** if a real supplier quote adds more than $10 to hoodie landed cost, the $135 price gives only 28.7% margin. Raise to $150 or drop the rhinestones to a smaller motif.
