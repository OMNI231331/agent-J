# Board summary: NORTHLINE

**Vote: 1 fund if, 2 pass. Average score 3.3/10** (Offers 3, Monopoly 2, Product 5).
Inputs for brand name, customer, budget and costs are assumptions; see `founder/idea.md`.

## Risks raised by more than one member (first)
1. **No proof the product is good**: no samples, supplier quotes, real photos or reviews (all three).
2. **No way to reach buyers**: "social/word of mouth" is not a channel; no audience, no cost per customer (Offers, Monopoly).
3. **Unvalidated customer**: the 18-34 target is an assumption, not a niche anyone can own first (all three).
4. **Unit costs and margin unknown**: no supplier numbers (all three) -> `/founder-cfo`.
5. **Weak edge**: "good fabric, fair prices" is easy to copy and there is no offer, guarantee or fit promise (Offers, Monopoly).

## Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] Launch with one hero product (the tee) plus at most two others (Product)
- [ ] Physical samples from a factory; test wash, fit and wear (Product)
- [ ] Real photos of real samples, no mockups (Product, Offers)
- [ ] Connect checkout; run one full test order through delivery and a return (Product)
- [ ] Name a narrow first market and a tested way to reach 100 buyers at a known cost (Monopoly, Offers)
- [ ] Real supplier quotes with margins; a guarantee the margin can afford (Offers)
- [ ] Demand evidence from outside the founder's circle: waitlist or pre-orders (Offers)
- [ ] A one-sentence pitch a stranger can repeat back (Product)

## Where the members disagree
The Product lens sees a salvageable business if it narrows to a hero product and gets real samples. The Offers and Monopoly lenses say that, as pitched, it is a generic basics brand with no edge and no distribution, and would pass until a narrow market, a real secret and a tested acquisition channel exist.

## The strongest version the board can see
A small drop of one hero product (the heavyweight tee), sold to a specific community the founder already belongs to (a school, team or scene), with real photos, a clear fit-and-quality guarantee, and a pre-order or waitlist to prove demand before any inventory is bought. This differs from the pitch: it is one product and one community, not eight products for everyone.
