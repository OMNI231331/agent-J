# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The anchor product is clear: Heavyweight Tee at $38 (240gsm organic cotton, boxy), with a stated fabric-quality angle.
- A tight range of 8 products keeps the offer simple and cheap to explain.
- A storefront already exists, so there is something to put in front of buyers.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No offer beyond the product.** "Quality basics at fair prices" is a price-and-fabric pitch. The lens says not to compete on price, and nothing here makes saying no feel foolish: no guarantee, bonus, fit promise or easy returns is stated.
2. **Likelihood of the outcome is unproven.** There are no real photos, no supplier quotes, no reviews and no payment processing. A buyer cannot trust that the tee fits, lasts or ships. Proof is the weakest side of the value equation here.
3. **The market is unvalidated and hard to reach.** The target (18-34, style-conscious) is an assumption. The brief states no existing audience and no way to reach buyers cheaply. Pain, spending power and growth are all unknown.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly buys this first, and what pain does a basic tee solve for them that existing options do not?
- What is the dream outcome: fit, durability, identity, or something else?
- What proof will buyers see (photos, reviews, wear tests, fabric tests)?
- What guarantee will you offer, and can you afford it at your unit cost? (Unit cost, margin and minimum order sizes are open numbers.)
- How will the first 100 buyers find you, and at what cost each? (Open.)
- Could you double the Heavyweight Tee price and still be the obvious choice? Why?
- How long from order to delivery, and what effort does the buyer carry (sizing, returns)?
- Would a small bundle or starter set make a stronger stack than eight separate items?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable at this vote. To reconsider, I would need: a named narrow buyer group, real supplier quotes with margins, real product photos, a stated guarantee, and evidence of demand such as a waitlist or pre-orders from people outside the founder's circle.
