## The idea

- What it is: a direct-to-consumer online clothing brand selling a small, tight collection of everyday basics (tees, hoodies, jeans, joggers, jackets, a cap).
- Who it is for (ASSUMPTION, founder to confirm): style-conscious men and women 18-34 who buy basics online and care about fabric quality and fair prices.
- What it sells, at what price: 8 products, $28 (cap) to $160 (light puffer). Anchor product: Heavyweight Tee $38. Others: Classic Hoodie $78, Pocket Long Sleeve $52, Relaxed Jeans $92, Everyday Joggers $64, Coach Jacket $120, Washed Cap $28.
- Where and how: website only (Next.js storefront in this repo; checkout not connected yet). Customers found via social/word of mouth. Made by small factories, shipped to the customer.
- Budget and constraints (ASSUMPTION): student founder, small budget (a few thousand dollars), no store, no staff, no existing audience stated.

## Summary

NORTHLINE is an online-only clothing brand with 8 everyday basics priced $28 to $160. The board voted 1 fund-if, 2 pass (average 3.3/10). The simulated buyer panel (20 buyers, a first look) bought 0 of 20: 15 passed on **trust** (unknown brand, no reviews or photos, can't try it on, unsure of returns) and 5 on price. The numbers work only if the ramp assumption holds (about 4 orders a day), and nothing in the panel supports it yet. The brand name, customer, budget and every cost are assumptions.

**Biggest next moves:** narrow to one hero product, get real samples and photos, add free returns and a size chart, and run a real-world test (waitlist or pre-order with a community) before buying inventory.

## What the board said

**Vote: 1 fund if, 2 pass. Average score 3.3/10** (Offers 3, Monopoly 2, Product 5).

### Risks raised by more than one member (first)
1. **No proof the product is good**: no samples, supplier quotes, real photos or reviews (all three).
2. **No way to reach buyers**: "social/word of mouth" is not a channel; no audience, no cost per customer (Offers, Monopoly).
3. **Unvalidated customer**: the 18-34 target is an assumption, not a niche anyone can own first (all three).
4. **Unit costs and margin unknown**: no supplier numbers (all three) -> `/founder-cfo`.
5. **Weak edge**: "good fabric, fair prices" is easy to copy and there is no offer, guarantee or fit promise (Offers, Monopoly).

### Conditions (checklist for the rest of the pack)
- [ ] Launch with one hero product (the tee) plus at most two others (Product)
- [ ] Physical samples from a factory; test wash, fit and wear (Product)
- [ ] Real photos of real samples, no mockups (Product, Offers)
- [ ] Connect checkout; run one full test order through delivery and a return (Product)
- [ ] Name a narrow first market and a tested way to reach 100 buyers at a known cost (Monopoly, Offers)
- [ ] Real supplier quotes with margins; a guarantee the margin can afford (Offers)
- [ ] Demand evidence from outside the founder's circle: waitlist or pre-orders (Offers)
- [ ] A one-sentence pitch a stranger can repeat back (Product)

### Where the members disagree
The Product lens sees a salvageable business if it narrows to a hero product and gets real samples. The Offers and Monopoly lenses say that, as pitched, it is a generic basics brand with no edge and no distribution, and would pass until a narrow market, a real secret and a tested acquisition channel exist.

### The strongest version the board can see
A small drop of one hero product (the heavyweight tee), sold to a specific community the founder already belongs to (a school, team or scene), with real photos, a clear fit-and-quality guarantee, and a pre-order or waitlist to prove demand before any inventory is bought. This differs from the pitch: it is one product and one community, not eight products for everyone.

## Pricing

From the 20-buyer simulated panel (price answers mostly about the $38 Heavyweight Tee), via `van_westendorp.py`:

- Acceptable range: **$15.11 to $42.06**. Least-resistance price (OPP) $30.16; indifference price $27.87.
- The $38 tee is inside the range but near the top. Median "getting expensive" answer is $35, "too expensive" $55.
- Price was the main reason for 5 of 20 passes, mostly students and fast-fashion shoppers comparing to Uniqlo, H&M and Shein ($13k-$25k incomes). For 15 of 20 the reason was trust, not price.
- **Recommendation to test (not a conclusion):** keep $38 for the tee, add a 3-pack or bundle (about $50 for 3 was suggested) and test a $32 launch price against $38 with real people. Cutting price will not fix the trust gap.
- Simulated buyers lean agreeable and anchor on the tee; use this to pick tests, not as a forecast.

## The numbers

- **The margin:** each order leaves $21.75 (40%) after its own costs; 29% margin at the planned 4 orders a day. All costs are estimates.
- **The line to watch:** the ramp. The plan assumes 4 orders a day by month 10, but the buyer panel bought 0 of 20. At half that volume the year-1 profit falls to $2,203 and the startup spend is not earned back. A 10% price cut (to $49.50) cuts year-1 profit to $7,444.
- **Cash:** about $4,421 before it pays for itself (startup $4,000 plus early losses).
- **Three ways to improve the margin:** (1) hold price and cut unit costs: a 15% cost rise costs $4,863 of year-1 profit, so supplier quotes matter most; (2) lower returns (free exchanges with a size chart should cut the 8% allowance); (3) sell bundles to lift order value above $55, since shipping is fixed per order.
- **Board conditions about money:** not yet met. No real supplier quotes or margins exist (see `founder/board.md`).
- An accountant should check structure, payroll and tax before money moves. This is not financial, tax or legal advice.
