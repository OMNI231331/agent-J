## 2. Round size and terms

**Amount now: $0 of outside money.** Fund the validation tests ($150 to $200 each, validate-idea.md) from savings. Total for the three experiments: about $450 (Estimate: 150 + 100 + 200).

**Instrument options, cheapest first**

| Option | Size | Cost to founder | Fit for LSW now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid pre-orders on the LSW store | 20 hoodies x $135 = $2,700 (Estimate, at the test price) | None. Refund if cap not met | Best. Customers fund the inventory |
| Friends and family | A few hundred to a few thousand | Strained relationships if it fails | Only as a written loan or gift, sized to what can be lost. Not equity |
| Crowdfunding (Kickstarter) | Varies | Platform fee, video and ad time | Weak. Fashion succeeded about 29% of the time vs about 43% overall, per [Banklesstimes](https://www.banklesstimes.com/?p=13372) and [Crowdcrux](https://www.crowdcrux.com/?p=19769), undated secondary sources. Needs an audience first |
| SBA microloan | Up to $50,000; average about $13,000 to $16,124 | Interest typically 8% to 13%, terms up to 6 or 7 years ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/small-business/sba-microloans), [Gusto](https://gusto.com/resources/articles/business-finance/funding/sba-microloan-program)) | Later, after a second drop. Secondary sources only, confirm on sba.gov. Lenders want a plan and often credit history |
| Equity (SAFE) | Not recommended now | Dilution plus investor expectations of 10x growth | No |

**If equity later:** a post-money SAFE is the standard instrument. About 91% of SAFEs on Carta in Q2 2026 were post-money ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/safe-valuation-caps-q2-2026/)).
- Valuation: Carta median cap for SAFEs under $250,000 was $7.5 million in Q2 2025; for $250,000 to $1 million rounds about $10 million (same Carta page and the [Q2 2025 report](https://carta.com/data/state-of-pre-seed-q2-2025/)). That is all-sector, mostly software. Apparel pre-seed caps: Not found. Do not expect those numbers for a pre-revenue clothing brand.
- Dilution: Estimate: a $150,000 raise on a $7.5 million post-money cap is 150,000 / 7,500,000 = 2%. A $150,000 raise at a $2 million cap would be 7.5%. Both are illustrations, not offers.
- Runway: $150,000 buys roughly 3 to 4 small drops if each drop costs about $15,000 to $40,000 (Estimate, not quoted). Inventory spend, not salary, drives the runway.

## 3. Investor targeting framework

**Investor profile for LSW, when it is ready**
- Fund stage: pre-seed or seed, or angels. Revenue-based financing once there are sales.
- Check size: $25,000 to $250,000 (Estimate: sized to a few drops).
- Sector focus: consumer brands, apparel, streetwear, culture.
- Geography: same country as the brand, ideally near a fashion hub.
- Style: hands-on in brand, retail and wholesale intros. Passive money from a general tech fund does not understand inventory risk.

**Warm intro tactics**
- Find 10 streetwear founders who started small. Ask each one specific question about sourcing, then ask who funded their first run.
- Buy a pre-order customer base first. A buyer who owns a hoodie and posts it is a better intro than a cold email.
- Ask each supplier and printer which brands they saw grow and who backed them.
- Church, youth ministry and Christian creative networks: ask for angels who have backed a business before. Do not pitch donors as investors.

## 5. Twelve-week plan (bootstrapping first)

Starts with the validation experiment from validate-idea.md. Dates are from the day the founder starts.

- **Week 1:** Run Experiment 3: ask 5 makers for 50-unit quotes and a crystal-work minimum. Check the LSW trademark. Open the real checkout for Experiment 1.
- **Weeks 2 to 3:** Run Experiment 1: 20-unit hoodie pre-order, $150 ads, full payment, refund if cap missed. Run Experiment 2 alongside ($50 per message version). Pass lines: 10 paid, 5 from strangers; 100 sign-ups.
- **Week 4:** Decide on the numbers. Under 3 paid orders: refund, change the price or message, rerun. Order one sample if quotes work.
- **Weeks 5 to 6:** If the cap hit, order production from pre-order cash only. Email buyers every week. Collect 2 to 3 testimonials, written with permission.
- **Weeks 7 to 8:** Ship. Record real landed cost, returns and contribution margin per order.
- **Weeks 9 to 10:** Open the second drop with a higher price if 90% sold in 14 days (pricing-strategy.md rule). Review the five metrics.
- **Weeks 11 to 12:** Funding review. If the readiness re-check in section 1 is not met, apply for no equity. If a loan is still wanted, talk to an SBA microloan intermediary with two drops of sales records.

## 6. Mistakes to avoid

1. **Raising before proof.** Founders want cash to start. Instead, sell first; a paid pre-order beats a pitch.
2. **Buying inventory with the budget before demand.** Founders want the product to look real. Instead, cap pre-orders and produce after payment.
3. **Chasing tech VCs.** They are easy to find. Instead, target consumer and apparel backers, or skip VC and use revenue.
4. **Pitching the faith story without numbers.** It is the part founders care about most. Instead, lead with sell-through and margin, and let faith be the reason buyers return.
5. **Taking family money on vague terms.** It feels informal. Instead, write the amount, repayment and what happens if the drop fails.
