# LSW buyer interviews: 25-minute Mom Test script

## Assumptions

- Assumption: the founder is solo, has no email list or following, and has never run an interview (facts.md, product-brief.md).
- Assumption: buyer age band is 18 to 28 and the price band to probe is $150 to $225 (persona-gen.md). Never state an LSW price in an interview.
- No new outside facts are used. Incentive amounts are proposals.

## 1. Objectives: the 3 assumptions to test

Taken from validate-idea.md (Q1, Experiment 2) and persona-gen.md ("what needs real people").

1. **Buyers want faith and streetwear in one garment.**
   - Confirmed: at least 4 of 6 describe a time they wanted a piece that carried belief and looked like real streetwear, and name what they did about it.
   - Rejected: they keep faith and fashion separate on purpose, or cannot recall a time it bothered them.
2. **Buyers pay $150 or more for an unknown brand.**
   - Confirmed: they describe a real purchase at or above $150 from a small or new brand, with the reason they trusted it.
   - Rejected: their highest recent purchase from an unknown brand is well under $150, or they say they only pay that for known names.
3. **Fit, fabric and return worries stop the sale, and proof can remove them.**
   - Confirmed: they tell a specific story of a return or a regretted order, and name the proof that would have prevented it.
   - Rejected: they do not recall returns or fit problems.

## 2. Screening criteria

- **Must have:** age 18 to 28; bought at least 1 hoodie, tee or pants from a streetwear or drop brand in the last 6 months; paid at least $60 for one item; follows fashion, sneakers or music on Instagram or TikTok.
- **Nice to have:** used a drop alert; has returned online clothing; has bought from a small brand.
- **Disqualify:** friends and family (they lie to be kind); anyone in apparel or design (they critique the product, not their behavior).
- **Mix (6 to 8 people):** 3 faith-open, 2 faith-neutral, 1 to 2 who dislike overt religion.
- **Incentive:** a $15 gift card for 25 minutes. Use a card, not a discount code, so the reward does not depend on liking you.

## 3. Where to find 5 to 8 people with no audience

Ask for a conversation, not a sale.

- **Youth groups and campus ministries:** ask the leader first. Message: "I'm a student/founder researching how people your age buy clothes. 25 minutes, $15 card, no pitch. Could I share this with your group or ask 2 members?"
- **Sneaker and streetwear communities:** Reddit threads, Discord drop-alert servers, local resale meetups. Read the rules, get mod approval where promotion is banned, and take part for a week before asking.
- **Referrals:** after each interview, ask for 2 recent streetwear buyers.

Outreach rules:
- One message, one follow-up after 3 days, then stop.
- Say who you are, the length, the incentive, and that you are not selling.
- Personalize each opening line. No copy-paste blasts.
- Accept a no at once.

Message template: "Hi [name], I'm building a clothing brand and talking to streetwear buyers about how they decide. Not selling anything. 25 minutes, $15 gift card. Would [day] or [day] work?"

## 4. Interview script

### Opening (2 min)

Say: "Thanks for doing this. I'm learning how people buy clothes, so there are no right answers. I'm not selling anything today. Honest and negative answers help me most, and you will not hurt my feelings. May I record the audio so I can focus on listening? It stays with me and I delete it after I take notes."

### Warm-up (3 min)

1. "Tell me a bit about what you do and what you're into these days."
2. "How do you usually find out about clothes you end up wanting?" Follow-up: "Which accounts, people or friends?"
3. "What are you wearing most this month, and where did it come from?"

### Problem exploration (10 min)

Past behavior only. Do not mention faith until question 6.

1. "Tell me about the last time you bought a hoodie, tee or pants from a streetwear brand." Follow-ups: "How did you find it? What else did you look at? What made you pick that one?"
2. "What did you pay, and how did that price feel at the time?" Follow-up: "Did you hesitate? What settled it?"
3. "Walk me through the last drop or limited release you tried to buy." Follow-ups: "What happened? Did you get it? What did you do when it sold out?"
4. "Tell me about the last time clothes you ordered online didn't work out." Follow-ups: "What was wrong: fit, fabric, look? Did you return it? What did that cost you in time or money?"
5. "Before you buy from a brand you don't know, what do you check?" Follow-ups: "Measurements? Reviews? Photos on real people? Return policy?"
6. "Think about the clothes you own. Does what you believe, faith or otherwise, show up in anything you wear?" Follow-ups: "Tell me about one piece. Where did you get it? What do you like or dislike about it?"
7. "Have you ever bought or passed on something because of a message on it?" Follow-up: "What was the message, and what made you decide?"

### Solution exploration (5 min)

Still no mention of LSW.

1. "If you could design your ideal hoodie from scratch, what would be on it and what would it feel like?" Listen for unprompted faith, crystals, fit, weight.
2. "What is the first thing you look at in photos to decide if a hoodie is worth it?"
3. "What would make you walk away from a brand even if the piece looked good?" Listen for dealbreakers.
4. "What have you tried so far to find clothes that match your taste and your beliefs?" Follow-up: "What was missing?"

### Reaction (5 min)

Say: "I'm making dark, oversized, heavyweight streetwear in small numbered runs, with original cross and star details. The design leads and the faith meaning is in the details."

1. "In your own words, what is that?" (Checks if they understood it.)
2. "What's your first reaction, including anything that bothers you?"
3. "Who do you know who wears something like this? Who definitely wouldn't?"
4. "What would have to be true for you to buy a piece from a brand like this?"

Real interest: specific questions about fabric, sizing or timing; asking where to see it.
Polite interest: "Cool, I love that," with no detail.

### Closing (2 min)

1. "Who else bought streetwear recently that I should talk to? Could you introduce me?"
2. Commitment test: "Would you be willing to join a sign-up list and give feedback on mockups this week?" Better, for strong signals: "Would you look at a pre-order page and tell me what you'd change?"
3. Say: "Thank you. Your gift card will arrive within 24 hours. I'll send mockups only if you say yes."

Never say a price or defend the design.

## 5. Analysis framework

After each call, fill one row within 30 minutes.

- **Signal vs. noise:** a pattern counts when 4 of 6 or 5 of 8 interviewees give the same behavior (a purchase, a return, a check they made), not the same opinion. One loud outlier is a quote, not a pattern. Tag each note "did" or "said". Only "did" counts.
- **Weigh the faith split:** if devout and open interviewees are pulled but neutral ones are pushed away, the message is niche. Compare by group.
- **Decision criteria:**
  - Build (go to Experiment 1, the paid pre-order): 4 of 6 or more confirm assumption 1, 3 or more name a purchase of $150 or more from a small brand, and at least 3 give a real commitment (a sign-up or a page review).
  - Pivot: the look pulls but the faith message pushes away 2 or more neutral interviewees. Lead with the look.
  - Kill or pause: fewer than 2 can recall a purchase near the price band, or nobody gives a commitment.
  - Fewer than 5 interviews: keep going.

## 6. Red flags cheat sheet

Polite, not honest:
- "That sounds cool" with no reason.
- "I'd definitely buy that." Future tense is unreliable.
- "You should add [feature]." They are designing for you, not describing a problem.
- They cannot describe the last time they bought or returned something.

Real interest:
- They describe a specific fit or fabric failure and what it cost them.
- They ask when and where they can see it.
- They offer introductions.
