# Drop Mechanics: Pre-launch, Drop-day and Post-drop Site Patterns for Limited Streetwear (for LSW DROP 001)

> Method note: direct page fetches (gov.uk, ftc.gov, legislation.gov.uk, hypebeast.com, help.shopify.com) were blocked by the sandbox egress proxy. Every finding below comes from search-result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Before publishing, check the primary texts, especially the legal ones. Brand claims are limited to what a source reports. Where a brand's site behaviour could not be sourced, it is listed under Gaps.

## Pre-launch: password pages, countdowns, waitlists/SMS, early-access tiers, teasers

### Takeaway
The strongest documented pre-launch pattern is gating plus a fixed, predictable release time. Corteiz uses a password-protected store with codes shared socially. Represent unlocks app-first or loyalty-tier early access with a password or link. Supreme, Kith, Palace and Sp5der publish fixed weekly or daily release times. For a small brand, the closest equivalent is an email/SMS early-access list that receives a password or link shortly before a published public time.

### Cited Findings
- **Corteiz, password-gated store.** BoF says Corteiz sells "via a password-protected ecommerce site or gamified physical drops" in London, New York and Paris. It credits a scarcity strategy and a private Instagram (860k+ followers at the time) for the air of exclusivity — [BoF](https://www.businessoffashion.com/community/people/clint)
- Euronews (Mar 2023) says site visitors hit a password prompt and that the brand's Instagram was once private. It attributes Corteiz's popularity to word of mouth and social media, not resale — [Euronews 2023](https://www.euronews.com/2023/03/17/the-rise-of-corteiz-inside-the-genius-marketing-strategies-of-londons-hottest-streetwear-b)
- A third-party ad-tracking site (low authority) describes drop codes released only on social and says the brand does not advertise — [AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/brands/corteiz?geo=US). Complex describes GPS-coordinate drops and community-first stunts — [Complex](https://www.complex.com/tag/clint-ogbenna)
- When Corteiz's Instagram briefly disappeared, the founder pointed followers to WhatsApp. This shows the brand keeps a backup owned channel — [Complex](https://www.complex.com/style/a/tracewilliamcowen/corteiz-instagram-mysteriously-removed)
- **Represent, app-first and password-locked web.** Per the app vendor's case study (self-interested source), launches often happen on the app first, sometimes hours or a full day before the web. During that window the website is temporarily password-locked, and a push notification carries a password or special link — [Venn Apps case study](https://www.vennapps.com/case-studies/represent)
- Represent loyalty: "Prestige" upper-tier members get early access to weekly limited drops — [eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/lessons-in-fashion-loyalty-with-represent-clothing/). A deal aggregator lists app perks of early access and priority notifications (unverified) — [Groupon](https://www.groupon.co.uk/discount-codes/represent-clothing)
- **Supreme.** Weekly drop at 11am Thursday (NY time for US, London for EU). Weekly items are announced on the site's news page, usually Mondays, with some collaborations revealed as late as Wednesday evening — [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/how-to-buy-supreme-clothing/). A season "preview" (lookbook/product grid) goes up before the season, and preview items release over the following weeks — [r/supremeclothing FAQ mirror](https://nyc1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/supremeclothing/wiki/faq). Note: these sources are mostly pre-2023, and the EU timing conflicts across sources (Forward2me says 4pm London) — [Forward2me](https://www.forward2me.com/retailers/supreme-community/)
- **Kith "Monday Program."** New products every week at 11am EST across shops, web and the Kith app — [Kith](https://kith.com/collections/kith-monday-program). A Fall 2026 announcement gave simultaneous 11AM EST/GMT/CET/JST online and app release — [Particl summary](https://www.particl.com/kith/kith-fall-2026-accessories-1000835-8f8df7b6)
- **Palace.** Spring 2026 Drop 11: 17 April at 11:00 BST for UK/EU/US and the next day for JP/KR/CN/AU, via webstore and stores — [Hypebeast DE 2026](https://hypebeast.com/de/2026/4/palace-skateboards-spring-2026-drop-11-release-info). Older (2017–18) coverage confirms the long-running Friday 11:00 BST pattern — [NSS Mag](https://nssmag.com/en/fashion/16160/palace-skateboards-fall18-drop-2)
- **Sp5der (May 2026).** A "Worldwide Five Day" event with daily web releases at 12:00 PM EST on its store, plus a one-day NYC pop-up with an in-store-only capsule — [Hypebeast 2026](https://hypebeast.com/2026/5/sp5der-worldwide-five-day-nyc-popup) (search summary only)
- **SMS early-access performance (vendor data).** HOMAGE subscribers who opted into early-access releases clicked SMS at 64%, against a 10.5% average — [Klaviyo/HOMAGE](https://www.klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/homage-apparel). Sneaker Politics' hottest drops "can sell out in about a minute". Before adding SMS, drop alerts went out only by email and social and didn't always reach its best customers. Klaviyo reports 133% growth in owned-marketing revenue — [Klaviyo/Sneaker Politics](https://klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/sneaker-politics-sms-marketing)
- **Third-party launch cadence (not streetwear-specific).** A VIP email+SMS goes out 3 to 1 days before launch, then an SMS to consenting subscribers within 30–120 minutes of public launch. The same guide recommends stating purchase limits up front — [Attribuly](https://blog2.attribuly.com/klaviyo-best-practices-limited-edition-jewelry-launch/)

### Inferences
- LSW's existing early-access email signup maps onto the Corteiz and Represent pattern. Gate the DROP 001 page behind a password or tokenized link, email it to the list a set window before a public time, then open publicly. That makes the early-access tier real and checkable rather than implied.
- The countdown should point to a fixed, published release time (as Supreme, Kith and Palace do), not a rolling or per-visitor timer. A countdown to a true launch moment is the low-risk use of a timer (see legal section).
- Supreme's "news page and preview before season" pattern supports a teaser or lookbook reveal on the DROP 001 page ahead of sale, followed by a precise date and time.
- Keep a second owned channel (email plus SMS or WhatsApp). The Corteiz Instagram incident shows the risk of a single social channel.

### Gaps
- Could not verify whether Corteiz, Hellstar or Sp5der use on-site countdown timers, or which SMS provider any of them use. No reliable source was found.
- No reliable source on Hellstar's website drop mechanics. Results were low-quality SEO pages.
- Postscript was not covered by any source found. Only Klaviyo case studies surfaced.

## Drop day: availability display, stock indicators, sold-out states, queues, bots, traffic

### Takeaway
High-demand stores pair a waiting room (queue) with checkout-level bot protection and purchase caps. On Shopify that is Plus-only bot protection (scheduled events of up to 60 minutes) plus third-party waiting rooms such as CrowdHandler or Cloudflare. A custom Next.js stack can reproduce this with Vercel BotID on the checkout route, a queue/waiting room, and atomic Redis reservations. Stock indicators are legal only when they reflect real inventory.

### Cited Findings
- **Shopify bot protection.** It is a Shopify Plus feature enabled through Plus support and then scheduled from the admin. It "blocks known bots from your checkout, and slows down bot activity so that your customers have the opportunity to make purchases during flash sales." Constraints: each event lasts at most 60 minutes, only one event can be scheduled at a time, there is a 15-minute gap between events, and it covers the Online Store channel only. It protects all products or selected ones (50 in the English doc, 500 in the Dutch doc — conflict). Shopify says it is only for limiting auto-checkout bots, not related fraud — [Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/bot-protection); [NL version](https://help.shopify.com/nl/manual/checkout-settings/bot-protection)
- **CrowdHandler waiting room (Shopify app).** Queues visitors during spikes, offers priority codes for VIP or pre-sale customers, syncs stock to prevent overselling, and blocks queue bypass. Its anti-cheat can stop bypassers from adding to cart and can cancel or refund orders that skipped the queue. It conflicts with accelerated-checkout buttons on product pages — [CrowdHandler blog](https://crowdhandler.com/blog/crowdhandler-waiting-room-now-available-in-the-shopify-app-store); [CrowdHandler flash-sale guide](https://www.crowdhandler.com/docs/80001187610-complete-guide-planning-and-executing-a-flash-sale-on-shopify-using-crowdhandler)
- **Gatekeeper: Bot Protection (Shopify app, launched Oct 2025).** Checks buyers at checkout. Features: timed releases, inventory caps, eligibility rules (account age, purchase history), human verification and quantity controls — [PickYourApp listing](https://pickyourapp.com/products/gameon-gate-keeper)
- **Cloudflare Waiting Room.** A platform-agnostic queue, reported to require Business/Enterprise plans, with bot detection filtering traffic before the queue — summarized in [SoleSavy queue explainer](https://help.solesavy.com/en/articles/6978092-what-is-a-queue-system-and-how-does-it-work) (secondary)
- **Queue gaming.** A sneaker-community guide describes entering the Shopify queue early with a dummy item and swapping in the real product at release. Drop operators need to close this loophole — [SoleSavy](https://solesavy.com/?p=93819)
- **Vercel BotID for Next.js.** Install `botid`, wrap the config with `withBotId`, then register protected routes such as `POST /api/checkout` with `initBotId()` in `instrumentation-client.ts` (Next 15.3+), or use `<BotIdClient/>` on older versions. Call `checkBotId()` server-side before the purchase logic. Unregistered routes are treated as bots. It always returns `isBot=false` in development. Basic is available on all plans; Deep Analysis needs Pro or Enterprise — [Vercel docs](https://vercel.com/docs/botid/get-started); [This Dot Labs](https://www.thisdot.co/blog/vercel-botid-the-invisible-bot-protection-you-needed)
- **Sell-out speed context.** Sneaker Politics' hottest drops can sell out in about a minute — [Klaviyo](https://klaviyo.com/customers/case-studies/sneaker-politics-sms-marketing)
- **Legal bounds on stock indicators.** A message such as "only five left in stock" is not unlawful in itself, provided there is no misleading context — [RPC on CMA open letter](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2023/cma-open-letter-to-businesses-on-urgency-and-price-reduction-claims/). "Selling fast – only 3 left" is likely misleading if there is no real shortage, or if new stock will arrive shortly — [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/online-claims-that-put-unfair-pressure-on-consumers-new-uk-guidance-on-what-to-avoid)

### Inferences
- **LSW's Redis per-SKU stock, limit 3 per item.** This is already the core of an honest drop system. For drop day it needs:
  - an atomic decrement or reservation (Lua script or `DECRBY` with a floor check) at add-to-cart or checkout, with a TTL'd cart hold that releases on expiry;
  - the per-customer cap of 3 enforced server-side per account, email or payment fingerprint, not only in the UI;
  - BotID (or a similar check) on the checkout and reserve routes;
  - optionally, a simple waiting room. A Redis sorted set of tokens issued at T-0, admitting N per interval, can stand in for CrowdHandler or Cloudflare.
- **Showing the cap.** Stating "Limit 3 per customer" visibly on the product page matches the practice of stating purchase limits up front (Attribuly). It is a true, verifiable statement, not a pressure claim.
- **Stock indicators.**
  - Show "Low stock" or exact counts only when they come straight from the Redis value. Never fake or pad them.
  - Before running "only X left", decide whether DROP 001 will be restocked. If a restock is planned, a scarcity line is likely misleading under the CMA view.
- **Sold-out state.** Render it from the same Redis value: a disabled button plus a "Sold out" label. Avoid any "X people viewing" widget unless it is real per-product data (see the popularity rules below).
- **Queue fairness.** The early-access tier should use a priority-code or separate-window mechanism, as CrowdHandler's priority codes do, so it doesn't look like a back door.

### Gaps
- No source documents how Supreme, Palace, Corteiz or Kith visually render low-stock or sold-out states on product pages in 2023–2026, so it is not claimed here. A Wayback Machine check is recommended but was not possible here (blocked).
- Which queue vendor (if any) each named brand uses was not found in any reliable source.
- Shopify's general (non-Plus) built-in bot and rate-limit protection was not confirmed.

## Post-drop: sold-out pages, archives, restock notifications, "notify me"

### Takeaway
"Notify me" / back-in-stock flows are a standard tool (Klaviyo supports SMS and email back-in-stock flows tied to inventory feeds). Streetwear norms favour no restocks, which makes an honest "Sold out — archived" state plus a "notify me for DROP 002" capture more credible than restock alerts for a one-off drop. Archive and resale interest exists independently (e.g., a third-party agency selling Palace archive weekly).

### Cited Findings
- **Klaviyo back-in-stock flows.** They support SMS, and texts are "typically read within minutes". Only current SMS subscribers receive them. The feature requires Shopify, BigCommerce, PrestaShop, Magento 2, or an inventory-aware catalog synced by feed or API, which is relevant for a custom stack — [Klaviyo Help](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/7954040204827-How-to-use-SMS-in-your-back-in-stock-flows)
- **Supreme restocks.** One source says items are rarely restocked once released; another (a Reddit FAQ) says restocks are random — [Highsnobiety](https://www.highsnobiety.com/p/how-to-buy-supreme-clothing/); [Reddit FAQ mirror](https://nyc1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/supremeclothing/wiki/faq) (older sources, conflicting)
- **Sp5der, no restocks after sell-out.** Reported only by low-quality pages, so unverified — [source](https://test.lsdassociates.com/?p=288290)
- **Palace archive.** A London agency (Too Hot Limited) sells Palace archive garments weekly, with each item unique, which points to secondary-market demand for past drops — [HIGHXTAR](https://highxtar.com/en/london-agency-puts-palace-archive-garments-on-sale/) (date unclear)
- **Kith.** No official countdown or restock-notification feature was found in search; the app was cited as the channel — [Kith](https://kith.com/collections/kith-monday-program)

### Inferences
- **LSW post-drop page.** Keep the DROP 001 page live as an archive record: the lookbook, the edition size per piece, and "Sold out" per SKU. Swap the CTA to "Get notified for DROP 002", reusing the existing early-access signup.
- **Wording.** Only say "Notify me when back" if a restock is genuinely possible. Otherwise say "This piece won't be remade" (if true). This keeps the edition claim honest and checkable.
- **Custom notify flow.** Without Shopify, either push inventory to Klaviyo via catalog API or feed, or run a simple notify list in Redis keyed by SKU and send through the existing email provider.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2023–2026 source describing how Supreme, Palace, Corteiz or Hellstar display sold-out or archive pages on their own sites.

## Storytelling: drop numbers, limited-edition labels, numbered pieces, interior tags, brand pillars

### Takeaway
Industry practice puts the edition and authenticity story into the garment itself: a woven or printed edition label or patch, increasingly with an NFC chip linking to a verification or product-passport page (Balenciaga, Infobip/IUTD, reportedly Hellstar). Drops are numbered and named sequentially (Palace "Drop 11", Supreme "Week N"), and DROP 001 fits that convention.

### Cited Findings
- **Sequential drop naming.** Palace uses "Spring 2026 Drop 11" — [Hypebeast DE](https://hypebeast.com/de/2026/4/palace-skateboards-spring-2026-drop-11-release-info). Supreme's seasons are tracked as "Week 1…N" droplists — [JustFreshKicks SS22 Week 1](https://justfreshkicks.com/supreme-ss22-week-1/) (2022). Kith uses a named recurring "Monday Program" — [Kith](https://kith.com/collections/kith-monday-program)
- **Balenciaga.** In its limited Archive collection, an NFC chip in a patch on each garment links to a digital product passport that also authenticates the item — [NFCW](https://www.nfcw.com/?p=86905)
- **Infobip × IUTD Studios.** 300 limited hoodies, each with an NFC tag that can be scanned by phone to verify it is unique and authentic — [ICTbusiness](https://www.ictbusiness.biz/business/infobip-enters-street-fashion-by-launching-a-limited-edition-nft-hoodie)
- **Hellstar.** Fan or SEO guides (low reliability) say recent 2024/25 capsules carry NFC chips in the wash tag or hang tag, and that the woven neck label is a key authenticity check — [guide](https://slbubble.studentbridge.com/celebnews006/7-shocking-ways-to-spot-a-fake-gray-and-green-hellstar-hoodie-2024-ultimate-guide-0cqyt.html)
- **Label feasibility.** NFC chips can go into patches and into smaller woven or printed labels — [Redmark](https://redmark.it/en/can-there-be-technology-in-high-fashion-labels-and-patches/)
- **Represent Owners' Club.** The line's name and story come from vintage car owners' clubs, an example of a pillar or narrative told through a product line — [The Sole Supplier](https://thesolesupplier.co.uk/news/represents-latest-owners-club-drop-showcases-the-best-in-summer-styling/)
- **Corteiz.** Coverage frames its brand-building as world-building and rebellion (an "anti-establishment" pillar) — [Unruly](https://getunruly.com/articles/clint-419-corteiz-building-a-cult-brand-through-world-building-and-rebellion)

### Inferences
- **On the site.** Show "DROP 001" as a persistent identifier (hero, product titles, URL `/drop-001`). Give each SKU an explicit edition size ("Edition of N") that equals the real Redis starting inventory. That makes the "limited" label a number the brand can prove.
- **In the garment.** If pieces are individually numbered ("07/150"), mirror that on the site with an authenticity or verify page (an optional NFC or QR on the interior tag that resolves to `/verify/<serial>`). The physical tag and the site then tell the same story.
- **Brand pillars.** Present them as a short manifesto band on the drop page, as Represent's line narratives and Corteiz's world-building do. No specific source tested the visual layout of this.

### Gaps
- No source found on specific interior-tag designs for Corteiz, Supreme or Palace, or on how any named brand visually presents edition counts on product pages.
- Effectiveness data (conversion lift) for numbered pieces or NFC tags: none found.

## Consumer-protection limits on urgency: honest scarcity vs. dark patterns

### Takeaway
Across the US, UK and EU, the line is truthfulness and verifiability. Real limited editions, real stock counts and countdowns to a real launch or end time are allowed. Fake or resetting timers, false low-stock or "selling fast" claims, false popularity claims, and "ends soon" offers that recur are the targets. The UK is now actively enforcing this (Emma Sleep consent order, May 2026, under DMCC-era powers).

### Cited Findings
- **FTC staff report, "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (Sept 2022).** Under "Design Elements that Induce False Beliefs" it lists false scarcity claims, countdown timers implying a nonexistent time limit, fake clocks that vanish or reset at zero, "nearly sold out" claims when stock is ample, and false "others are viewing or buying" claims. Liability turns on the "net impression" of the site's design elements. As a staff report it guides enforcement but is not binding rule-making — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/09/the-ftc-brings-more-light-to-dark-patterns-in-new); [Hunton](https://www.huntonprivacyblog.com/2022/09/27/ftc-releases-report-on-dark-patterns/) (pre-2023, flagged as older but still the operative FTC framework found)
- **UK CMA open letter (29 Mar 2023).** It covers "urgency claims": scarcity, popularity, "act fast" or time-limited claims including countdown clocks, with examples such as "Only 7 left" and "14 sold in the last 24 hours" — [SCL](https://www.scl.org/12845-cma-publishes-letter-to-businesses-about-online-redlines/); [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2023/cma-open-letter-to-businesses-on-urgency-and-price-reduction-claims/)
- **Countdowns (CMA).** A countdown is problematic if the deal continues past the stated end, recurs shortly after, or the timer resets on refresh or at zero — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2024/cma-investigates-unfair-online-sales-practices)
- **Scarcity (CMA).** "Selling fast – only 3 left" is likely misleading if there is no real shortage, or if new stock is arriving shortly — [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/online-claims-that-put-unfair-pressure-on-consumers-new-uk-guidance-on-what-to-avoid). A bare true stock statement is not unlawful in itself, absent misleading context — [RPC](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2023/cma-open-letter-to-businesses-on-urgency-and-price-reduction-claims/)
- **Popularity (CMA).** Problematic if the stated number of units was not sold in the stated time, or if the figures relate to a different product — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2024/cma-investigates-unfair-online-sales-practices)
- **UK legal basis.** Under the Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008, a distorting claim is likely an unfair commercial practice and a criminal offence. False time-limited claims are blacklisted in Schedule 1 — [Hogan Lovells](https://hlc.com/en/publications/online-claims-that-put-unfair-pressure-on-consumers-new-uk-guidance-on-what-to-avoid). The DMCC Act 2024 gave the CMA direct enforcement powers — [Ashurst](https://www.ashurst.com/en/insights/mind-the-nudge-the-dmccs-crackdown-on-manipulative-online-design/)
- **Emma Sleep.** The CMA filed in the High Court (17 Oct, reported as 2024) — [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/?p=175075). On 22 May 2026 the High Court approved a consent order with a penal notice, stopping countdown timers that implied promotions were expiring, "high demand" banners that overstated interest, and offers that were not truly time-limited — [Mondaq/Lewis Silkin](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/dodd-frank-consumer-protection-act/1796032/uk-consumer-law-revolution-emma-sleep-case-is-partly-settled-but-the-other-part-is-keeping-us-awake-at-night)
- **EU UCPD 2005/29.** Annex I is a blacklist of practices "in all circumstances" unfair, applied uniformly in all member states — [legislation.gov.uk copy](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2005/29/annexes/data.htm). Annex I point 7 (falsely stating that a product is available only for a very limited time to force an immediate decision) is from the researcher's prior knowledge and was **not verified** in this session; check it on EUR-Lex. The Commission opened a Digital Fairness Act consultation in July 2025, expected to update the UCPD; its current status was not found — [lawyersweek](https://lawyersweek.net/23877/brightening-the-path-for-understanding-certain-dark-patterns.html)
- **Consumer-side caution.** A collector guide warns that "exclusive", "limited" and "collector's edition" don't by themselves indicate genuine scarcity — referenced in the NFC/labels search results (source not individually verified)

### Inferences
**Honest-scarcity checklist for LSW**, derived from the rules above:

| Allowed (if true) | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Edition of N", where N matches the starting inventory | Restocking a "limited" piece without disclosure |
| Countdown to a fixed DROP 001 launch time that does not reset | Per-visitor or evergreen timers |
| Live stock counts read from Redis | Synthetic "X viewing now" or "sold in last hour" counters unless real and per-product |
| "Limit 3 per customer" | "Ends soon" early-access windows that quietly extend |

- If DROP 001 might be restocked, don't call it "never again", and avoid "only X left" urgency copy.
- Keep a log (Redis snapshots) so every stock or edition claim can be substantiated later, in line with the FTC "net impression" test and the CMA substantiation expectations.

### Gaps
- The CMA open letter text, the FTC report PDF and EU Annex I wording were not read directly (proxy blocked). Wording above comes from law-firm summaries.
- No US enforcement action specific to fashion-drop scarcity was found.
- The current status of the EU Digital Fairness Act proposal is unknown.

## Tools commonly used, and what a custom Next.js site needs to replicate them

### Takeaway
The Shopify drop stack is: Plus bot protection, a waiting-room app (CrowdHandler, Gatekeeper), Klaviyo email/SMS (with back-in-stock flows), and optionally an app-first early-access channel (Represent via Venn Apps). A custom Next.js + Redis site can cover most of it. LSW already has gating-ready signup, inventory, and per-SKU caps. It mainly needs bot checks (BotID), atomic reservation, a lightweight queue, and an email/SMS integration fed by its own inventory.

### Cited Findings
- Shopify Plus bot protection: scheduled, max 60 minutes, Plus only — [Shopify Help](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/checkout-settings/bot-protection)
- CrowdHandler: waiting room, priority codes, stock sync, anti-bypass — [CrowdHandler](https://crowdhandler.com/blog/crowdhandler-waiting-room-now-available-in-the-shopify-app-store)
- Gatekeeper: timed releases, inventory caps, eligibility rules, human verification — [PickYourApp](https://pickyourapp.com/products/gameon-gate-keeper)
- Klaviyo SMS + back-in-stock, which needs a supported platform or an inventory-aware catalog feed/API — [Klaviyo Help](https://help.klaviyo.com/hc/en-us/articles/7954040204827-How-to-use-SMS-in-your-back-in-stock-flows)
- App-first early access via a branded app vendor (Represent) — [Venn Apps](https://www.vennapps.com/case-studies/represent)
- Vercel BotID for Next.js route protection — [Vercel docs](https://vercel.com/docs/botid/get-started)

### Inferences
Custom Next.js equivalents for LSW, smallest first:

1. **Countdown.**
   - Render from one server constant (`DROP_001_AT`, UTC).
   - Gate server-side: the product API refuses purchases before T-0.
   - Never compute the time limit client-side per visitor.
2. **Early-access gate.**
   - Signed, expiring token links emailed to the signup list. A shared password works but leaks easily, which is the Corteiz and Represent trade-off.
   - Middleware checks the token or cookie before T-public.
3. **Bot protection.** BotID on the reserve and checkout routes, plus rate limiting by IP or account in Redis.
4. **Inventory.**
   - Atomic Redis reserve via a Lua script that checks per-user count ≤ 3 and stock > 0, with a TTL hold.
   - Commit on payment success, release on expiry.
   - This replaces Shopify stock sync.
5. **Queue (optional).** Only needed if traffic exceeds checkout capacity. Options: Cloudflare Waiting Room, or Redis token admission.
6. **Notify me / SMS.** Push the SKU catalog and inventory to Klaviyo via API, or keep a Redis set per SKU and send via the existing email provider. SMS needs explicit opt-in.
7. **Sold-out and archive.** Derive both from stock = 0. Keep the DROP 001 page permanently as an archive.

### Gaps
- Pricing for CrowdHandler and Cloudflare Waiting Room (2026) was not verified.
- Postscript features were not researched (no source surfaced).
- No case study was found of a small indie streetwear brand on a custom (non-Shopify) stack running drops.
